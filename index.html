A PROGRAM BUILT ON TRADITION

More than a century of Griz football.

Montana football began in 1897. What followed was a century-plus of championships, rivalries, legendary players, packed stadiums and postseason runs that made the Grizzlies one of the defining programs in the FCS.

THE TURNING POINT

Washington-Grizzly Stadium changed everything.

Montana opened Washington-Grizzly Stadium on Oct. 18, 1986, with a 38–31 comeback over Idaho State. The new home became the foundation for the modern Griz era — and one of the strongest home-field advantages in college football.

THE DYNASTY

1993–2009: the era that made the Griz a national power.

Montana reached the postseason a record 17 straight seasons from 1993–2009, won 15 conference titles during that span, played in seven national championship games and captured national championships in 1995 and 2001.

THE ROAD TO GREATNESS

Milestones that built Griz Nation

1897 → 2026
1897

It starts here.

Montana plays its first football season. The Grizzlies' first recorded season lays the foundation for what becomes one of the state's defining sports traditions.

1969

First Big Sky championship.

Montana wins the first Big Sky football championship in program history, beginning a conference tradition that would eventually produce a league-record 19 titles.

1986

Washington-Grizzly Stadium opens.

The Griz rally past Idaho State 38–31 in the first game at their new home. The stadium becomes the launchpad for the modern Montana football era.

1995

First national championship.

Don Read's Grizzlies go 13–2 and defeat Marshall 22–20 in the I-AA championship game. Quarterback Dave Dickenson wins the Walter Payton Award.

2001

Second national championship.

Joe Glenn's Griz finish 15–1 and beat Furman 13–6 in Chattanooga. Yohance Humphery runs for 142 yards and the defense nearly posts a championship-game shutout.

2008

Another title-game run.

Montana shocks top-seeded James Madison 35–27 in the semifinal to reach the national championship game for the sixth time, before falling to Richmond.

2009

Back to Chattanooga.

The Griz storm through the playoffs, including a 24–17 semifinal win over Appalachian State, before a 23–21 loss to Villanova in the championship game.

2023

19th Big Sky title. Eighth title game.

Montana beats Montana State 37–7 for the league championship, then wins two overtime playoff thrillers before reaching the national championship game for the first time since 2009.

2025

Another historic postseason.

Montana reaches the FCS semifinal for the 13th time — the most in FCS history — and extends its record total to 29 playoff appearances.

THE TWO CROWNS

Montana's national championships

1995

Montana 22, Marshall 20

Record: 13–2. Don Read's first title team won Montana's first national championship in a comeback for the ages. Dave Dickenson earned the Walter Payton Award and the Griz finished a perfect 8–0 in Big Sky play.

2001

Montana 13, Furman 6

Record: 15–1. Joe Glenn's team finished the job after reaching the title game the year before. Yohance Humphery scored the lone Griz touchdown and the defense forced three turnovers.

THE BRAWL OF THE WILD

Montana vs. Montana State

The rivalry began in 1897 and has become one of the oldest and most intense rivalries in Division I football. The 2025 season produced a historic first: the two teams met twice in the same season, including a playoff semifinal in Bozeman.
